### Fixed
- **Four real ball/pin physics bugs, found while chasing "the ball isn't hitting pins right."**
  - **Ball<->pin collision was discrete.** `BowlingBall` set `CollisionDetectionMode.Continuous`, which only sweeps against STATIC colliders — rigidbody-vs-rigidbody swept collision needs `ContinuousDynamic` on BOTH bodies. Pins were left at the Discrete default, so a fast ball could start in front of a pin and finish behind it in a single physics step (0.28m per step at 14 m/s vs. a 0.12m pin) with no error logged. Fixed by setting `ContinuousDynamic` on both `BowlingBall` and `Pin`; changing only one side does nothing, which is why the original code looked plausible and wasn't.
  - **The ball skidded instead of rolling.** `Launch()` correctly sets `angularVelocity = speed / radius`, but Unity silently clamps to the project's Default Max Angular Speed (50 rad/s) — true rolling at max power needs ~127 rad/s, so 60% of the spin was being discarded every throw. Raised per-rigidbody on the ball only.
  - **Pins spawned half-buried in the lane.** A pin's origin is its middle (the cylinder primitive's pivot), and the builder correctly offsets the template by `PinHeight * 0.5` — but `PinDeck.ResetFullRack` overwrote that with a bare lane-level position on every rack. Survivable with one chunky box collider (pins popped up 19cm and settled), but it silently consumed 0.19m of `Pin.IsStanding`'s 0.6m displacement budget on every single throw, and it started ejecting pins hard enough to read as "knocked" (and vanish via `ClearDeadWood`) once pins gained a thin base pad.
  - **Pins are now a compound collider — a capsule body on a small flat base pad — instead of one full-size box.** A box presents a flat wall at full width down to the lane, so the ball rode up it instead of glancing off, and a fallen box was a ramp the ball climbed rather than an obstacle it rolled past. New `PinConfig.UseShapedCollider`/`BaseDiameter01`. Centre of mass is now genuinely bottom-heavy via new `PinConfig.CenterOfMassHeight01` — the first implementation of this silently did almost nothing, because `Rigidbody.centerOfMass` is local-space and the pin's transform carries a non-uniform 0.19 scale, so a value expressed in metres was being shrunk to a fifth; now routed through `Transform.InverseTransformVector`.
  - Ball/pin **mass ratio** retuned by feel, not realism: real bowling's ~4:1 ball-to-pin ratio read as limp once the collision bugs above were fixed and pins were actually being hit. Cannonball's deliberately unrealistic ~17:1 ratio was the one that felt right, so the PIN got lighter (`PinMass 1.53 → 0.9`) rather than every ball getting heavier — consistent with this config's own long-standing "lighter pins fly further = funnier" design note.
  - New editor tool **`WeeSpurts > Retune Ball + Pin Physics`** stamps coherent ball/pin values with the reasoning attached and prints a before/after table. Speeds, green zones, timing-chaos curves, Nuke staging and Wobbler's weave were untouched — those stay feel, and stay Tony's.
- **`BallConfig.Radius` could make `HalfLaneWidth` negative and silently invert left/right aim.** `HalfLaneWidth = LaneWidth/2 - Radius`; an oversized ball (radius > half the lane) drove it negative, which flipped the sign on every consumer at once — the thrower's aim slide, the aim preview, and the resolved throw — with no visual clue, because the ball's rendered mesh scale is baked at scene-build time and doesn't track a config edited afterward. `BowlingGameController.HalfLaneWidth` now clamps to 0 (a ball as wide as the lane genuinely has nowhere to go sideways — dead aim is debuggable, backwards aim isn't) and logs a one-time error naming the exact config and the fix.
- QA-reviewed (qa-engineer agent) before merge, per CLAUDE.md: verdict safe to merge, no hallucinated Unity APIs, no client-trust gaps, no scoring-correctness bugs (`Pin.IsStanding`'s interaction with the collider/spawn changes was specifically checked). Five low-risk comment/doc-accuracy fixes applied.

### Added
- **`AlleyCirculationAudit` — the venue proves it is walkable, rather than asserting it.** A pairwise AABB gap test can tell you two boxes are 1.6m apart; it cannot tell you whether you can walk from the front door to the bar, whether the floor loops, or whether one gap is the only way somewhere. Those are connectivity questions, so this uses a connectivity algorithm: rasterise the floor into a 0.25m grid (131 × 80 ≈ 10,500 cells, milliseconds), flood fill it, and measure. Enforces Tony's **five** categories — **primary 4.0m, secondary 2.5m, threshold 1.2m**, plus **amenity interior 1.2m** and **back-of-house 1.2m**, the last two added by Tony after the first audit reported those gaps UNCLASSIFIED and asked him to categorise rather than guess — and reports, never fixes.
  - **The grid is 2.5D.** Each cell carries the height of the floor under it and two cells only connect if the step between them is walkable. That is what makes the sunken pits and the raised casino behave: the 0.4m recess is deliberately larger than `CirculationStepHeight` and the step rise deliberately smaller, so a pit is reachable *only* down its steps and a mis-sized tread reports the pit unreachable instead of silently passing.
  - **Categories and directions of travel are DECLARED by the builder, never inferred.** The builder knows a pit gap is a pit entrance and a doorway is a doorway, so it says so at the moment it builds them; likewise which way you travel through a zone, because guessing that from the rectangle's aspect ratio measures a wide shallow doorway's *length* instead of its width. Anything measured that no declaration covers is reported **UNCLASSIFIED** for Tony to categorise.
  - **Widths are measured per floor terrace, on the medial axis.** Two traps here, both hit and both fixed: run one distance transform over the whole reachable set and a sunken pit reads as open floor continuous with the concourse; and report the *narrowest cell* in a zone and you always find the nearest wall, so every route scores ~0.25m. Widths come from per-terrace transforms, and route widths from the corridor centreline, walking each zone along its declared travel direction.
  - **The loop check is exact**: the first Betti number of the reachable cell complex, which is literally the number of independent laps you can walk. Plus a **threshold-removal test** — delete each declared gap and re-run the fill — which answers "is this the only way in". Intentional dead ends (casino, card dealer) are exempt per Tony's rule.
  - Also checks anchor reachability from the front door (with the snap distance reported, so a buried anchor is visible rather than silently rescued), and 2.0m open-floor furniture spacing — the one check where a pairwise AABB test genuinely *is* the right tool.
- **Lane pitch for the venue is ~2m, with slim gutter rails on backdrop lanes only.** Tony's call: at `LaneConfig.NeighbourLaneSpacing`'s 2.9 (forced wide by the real lane's chunky 0.55m rail stand-off) the 12-lane bank alone ran 34.8m and the room 41×43m — a commute in a game where downtime is meant to be the fun part, and against the Wii reference where lanes read tight together. The real lane keeps its own geometry untouched; only the backdrop lanes get 0.3m gutters, which nobody reads at that distance. Any backdrop rail that would intersect the real lane's wider rails is skipped and the real rail serves both lanes. `LanePitch` stays a config field so it can be pushed back out if it reads cramped.
- **`GreyboxSceneBuilder`'s four cosmetic neighbour lanes are superseded** by the venue's full lane bank — they sit in the same slots and would overlap and z-fight. The venue builder destroys any `NeighbourLanes` root it finds and warns; the permanent fix is one untick of the **existing** `LaneConfig.BuildNeighbourLanes` tickbox, which was already there for exactly this. **`GreyboxSceneBuilder.cs` was not modified.**
- **Spin is now a 2D selector, not a single float — schema change to `LaunchParameters`.** `Spin` went from `float` (-1 left .. +1 right) to a `Vector2` clamped inside the **unit circle** (a circle, not a square, so a full diagonal isn't secretly 1.41× stronger than full sideways). Done now, deliberately, because networking doesn't exist yet and this is the cheapest moment it will ever be. `Vector2` rather than two floats because Mirror ships a built-in writer for it, so the networked version needs no custom serializer. **X = side spin / axis tilt = the hook. Y = roll vs skid**, a real gameplay axis: topspin grips and curves EARLY (straighter overall, plus a little forward drive), backspin SKIDS and then breaks LATE and harder. Q/E are gone.
  - **New `SpinModel.cs` — all the spin maths in one pure static file**, no Unity objects, no randomness, no `Time`. `BowlingBall` (real physics) and `AimPreview` (the preview line) both call it, so the preview and the throw **cannot** drift apart — the same failure `BowlingGameController.HalfLaneWidth` already exists to prevent for lateral start position, applied to the curve. It's also why the whole model is EditMode-testable with no scene, a Rigidbody or a play session.
  - **The vertical axis is two independent terms, on purpose.** `RampShape` decides *when* the curve happens (topspin `2(1-u)` front-loaded, neutral `1` flat, backspin `2u` back-loaded) and has a **mean of exactly 1.0 for every Y**, so it only ever redistributes curve over the lane and never adds any. `GripScale` decides *how much* there is (`1 - Y·RollSkidHookScale`; at the 0.6 default, topspin 0.4×, backspin 1.6×). Splitting them is what keeps the two knobs from secretly moving each other. Diagonals combine for free because the whole thing is a Lerp.
  - **The mistiming Hook (§8) is untouched and ADDITIVE.** Player spin and the release fumble are separate forces with separate config knobs (`SpinCurveForce` vs `HookForceMagnitude`), neither scaling the other, so they compete: dial full left and release hard and the ball still slices right — intent survives but gets mauled. The Hook is deliberately **not** ramped, so a fumble reads as a constant wrongness from the instant of release and stays visually distinguishable from a dialled late break. At the defaults (6 vs 4) intent wins by default; raise `HookForceMagnitude` toward `SpinCurveForce` for chaos-forward, lower it for skill-forward.
  - **Off-centre placement does NOT cost power** — a deliberate deviation from pool. Power stays entirely on the timing meter (Tony's call); two systems fighting over speed is exactly what this avoids. `SpinDriveForce` only ever *adds* forward push on topspin, never subtracts on backspin.
  - New `BallConfig` knobs: `SpinRampDistance` (18 m ≈ foul line to headpin), `RollSkidHookScale` (0.6), `SpinDriveForce` (3). `ConeSpinJitterMagnitude` now documented as horizontal-only — a fumble shoves the ball sideways, it doesn't change whether you rolled it or skidded it.
  - **Determinism preserved.** The seeded `System.Random` stream in `BowlingBall.Launch` keeps its three draws in the same order (angleJitter → spinJitter → wobblePhase), so an existing `Seed` still reproduces the identical wobble phase. Nothing in the new spin path reads `Time.time`, `UnityEngine.Random` or live input.
- **The aim preview line is now a physical prediction, not a hand-picked quadratic.** It spans the whole `SpinRampDistance` (was a fixed 2.5 m — 14% of the ramp, which made the late break literally invisible) and derives its curve from `SpinModel` + `F = ma` off the live `BallConfig`. Two honest approximations remain, both documented: constant forward speed (ignores drag, ~14% under-predicted drift) and an assumed green release, since power genuinely isn't known during AIM. It therefore previews your **intent**, not your fumble — the mistiming Hook is absent by design.
- **`CameraAnchor` is now a standalone scene-root object, shared by both thrower branches.** Previously a child of the thrower (and, in the capsule fallback, the thrower *itself*). With the character now sliding during aim, a parented anchor would drag every camera beat sideways each time you adjust lateral aim — and a frame that moves while you aim makes it harder to judge where you're pointing. Wii Sports keeps the aim camera fixed and lets the character slide within it (Tony's call). Not one `ThrowCameraSequenceConfig` value changed.

The load-bearing ones are the invariants, not the numbers: that `RampShape` has mean exactly 1.0 for every Y (if that drifts, the two vertical knobs stop being independent), and that `NormalizedDrift` really is the double integral of `RampShape`, checked by numerical integration rather than by trusting the comment — a wrong integral would make the preview *confidently* wrong instead of obviously broken.

### Fixed
- **QA pass (qa-engineer agent) on the above found five defects, all fixed before merge; two more were surfaced to Tony as decisions rather than patched.** The agent independently re-derived both spin-math invariants (mean-1 ramp: true; drift-is-the-double-integral: matches to 2.5e-6 at nine values of Y), confirmed determinism and the untouched RNG stream, confirmed the un-parented `CameraAnchor` genuinely cannot move, confirmed `TurnManager`/`BowlingScorer` were not touched, and found **no hallucinated APIs**.
  - **A transparency knob that silently did nothing.** `CharacterTransparentDepthWrite` was shipped as an A/B toggle, and it was a lie in the Inspector: **URP/Lit has no `_ZWriteControl` property** (verified — 0 occurrences in this project's `Lit.shader`; it only exists on ShaderGraph-authored URP shaders), and URP's own shader GUI forces ZWrite off for any transparent surface regardless. Tony would have flipped it, seen zero change, and concluded the transparency system was broken. The knob is **removed**, with the reasoning recorded so it isn't re-added.
  - **The transparency comment block was factually wrong about blend state.** It argued at length that `_ALPHAPREMULTIPLY_ON` must stay disabled for plain Alpha blending. The generated `Thrower_*.mat` proves URP does the opposite — `_ALPHAPREMULTIPLY_ON` enabled, `_SrcBlend: 1` (One), not the `SrcAlpha` the code sets — because URP's Lit defaults `_BlendModePreserveSpecular` on. Visually correct either way, but the code was not in control the way it claimed. Now documents that `_Surface`/`_Blend` are the real controls and URP derives the rest.
  - **Swapping the character model would have given the new body the old body's colours.** All eight Quaternius bodies share the same six material slot names (`Eyes, Hair, Pants, Shirt, Skin, Socks`), and the copied `.mat` filename was keyed on the slot name alone — so changing `CharacterModelPath` (which the tool's own doc comment explicitly invites) and re-running would reuse the previous body's materials silently: a suit rendered in casual-wear colours, nothing in the console. Filenames are now keyed to the model, and an existing copy is re-stamped from its source.
  - **The transparency path had no repair route — a deleted `.mat` bricked the character permanently.** The tool keyed off the remap map and treated "any entry present" as done. Delete one `Thrower_*.mat` and its entry resolves to null, the FBX no longer emits the original (it's remapped), so the tool warned "exposes no materials — check the Materials tab" (the wrong cause) and gave up, **forever**. A half-completed run had the same problem. It now drives off the FBX's own slot list, treats a missing target as a first-run, and clears an orphaned remap so the FBX can rebuild it.
  - **The spin dot could stick to the cursor and be dragged without ever clicking the widget.** `_dragging` was only cleared by a `MouseUp` the widget actually saw — but `OnGUI` early-returns before `HandleMouse` when the aim phase ends, and a release outside the Game view (Alt-Tab, drag onto the Inspector) never arrives at all. Latched, the *next* click-and-drag anywhere on screen would teleport the dot to the cursor and start tracking it, and the player would throw with spin they never dialled. Now cleared on aim-phase exit, on a `MouseDown` that misses the circle, and authoritatively via `Input.GetMouseButton(0)`. Also: left-button only (a right-click drag across the widget set spin), and `[Min(20f)]` on `widgetSize`, since 0 made `ApplyMouse` divide by zero and feed **NaN into a PhysX force**.
  - **`ThrowerAimSlide` carried stale damping velocity across turns** — `_slideVelocity` was never reset, so a velocity from an aim phase that ended mid-slide sat through the whole roll, pin count and turn change, then fed into the next aim as an overshoot from a stimulus seconds old. Zeroed at the end of the aim phase (deliberately not mid-slide — `SmoothDamp` owns that value between calls, and resetting it mid-slide is what makes damping snap).
- **Throw mechanic locked** (`GameBible.md` §8; feel-knobs stay open in `OpenQuestions.md`). Miss-timing on the power meter = *grounded human-fumble chaos*: bad timing distorts direction/spin so the thrower visibly screws up (never supernatural or world-breaking), good timing stays clean and predictable (the competitive hook). Escalation is **non-linear** — small misses mild, big whiffs spectacular — with named failure archetypes (Hook / Chip / Overcook / Whiff) hand-placed at the meter extremes. Skill = consistency and style, not scoreboard domination (pin scatter + betting/heckle/drink layers keep it level). Refined from an earlier Sonnet draft of the same idea.

### Fixed
- **The DEFAULT ball was flagged as a Nuke.** `BallConfig.asset` had `IsNuke: 1` written into the asset itself, so *every* throw resolved as a Nuke Shot — the tight `0.82`–`0.85` green zone instead of the normal `0.80`–`0.85`, and the explosion sequence in place of a physics roll. The `DebugHud` showed the correct config name, which made it read as "the game is broken" rather than "the ball is wrong": the flag lives on the asset, not on the selection. `GreyboxSceneBuilder` never stamps `IsNuke` onto the default config (it only ever sets it on `Nuke.asset`, and only on first creation), so this was hand-set in the Inspector during nuke testing. Cleared, and `DebugHud` now prints **`[NUKE ARMED]`** beside the ball name whenever the active config has the flag, so it can't hide again.
- **Nuke miss on a fresh frame desynced the scorer from the shared pin deck.** Ending the turn unconditionally after a whiff (without checking whether the frame's roll bookkeeping had actually finished) left the scorer expecting a second roll that never came — the next time that player was up, they'd inherit whatever pins the *other* player's last roll had left standing instead of a correctly re-racked frame. Fixed by walking the frame to actual completion (calling the existing `BowlingScorer.AddRoll` an extra time if needed, never touching its internals) before ending the turn. Provably bounded — traced every reachable state, worst case is one extra call.
- **Hook (and the matching Body English lean) hooked the wrong direction.** `Docs/OpenQuestions.md`/`Docs/BowlingFeelIdeas.md` spec "too HARD → hooks RIGHT, too SOFT → hooks LEFT"; the built code had it backwards since the mechanic was first added. Fixed the sign in both `BowlingBall.cs` and `CapsuleThrowReactionActor.cs` together (they read the same `TimingError01` sign independently — flipping only one would've made the thrower's lean and the ball's curve point opposite ways).
- **Green zone not updating on a mid-aim ball switch.** Switching to the Nuke ball *during* an already-started aim phase kept using the previous ball's (wider) green zone until the next roll — the zone only got pushed to `BallLauncher` at the start of a roll, not when the active config changed mid-aim. Fixed by re-applying the correct zone from `SetBallConfig` too, not just `BeginRoll`.
- **Wobbler force was ~30x too weak to be visible.** A sinusoidal force's peak velocity contribution is bounded by `F / (2π·frequency·mass)` — unlike a constant force (e.g. Hook), it doesn't keep accumulating over the roll. The original tuning (`3N` at `2.5Hz`) worked out to ~0.03 m/s of drift, imperceptible. Retuned to `12N` at `0.5Hz` (~0.64 m/s, and slow enough to read as one lazy weave instead of a fast shimmy).
- **Ball bounce material now uses `bounceCombine = Maximum`** (`GreyboxSceneBuilder`) so a ball's own `BallConfig.Bounciness` always wins over the lane floor's instead of averaging with it. Fixes bouncy-ball feel testing (ball was "slamming down" even at high `Bounciness`) and means future ball-feel powerup variants never need a matching floor material.
- **Switching `BallConfig` mid-session now actually changes bounciness.** `BowlingBall.Launch()` previously left the ball's physic-material bounciness at whatever the *default* `BallConfig` had baked in at scene-build time — swapping to `BouncyBall` via the sandbox switcher changed mass/speed/spin but not bounce. `Launch()` now restamps an instanced copy of the collider's physic material (`collider.material`, not `sharedMaterial`) with the active config's `Bounciness` on every throw. Also: `GreyboxSceneBuilder` now auto-seeds the switcher with `BouncyBall`/`Cannonball` if those assets exist, so they're selectable without manually dragging them into the Inspector.

### Changed
- **`LaneConfig` is lane-geometry-only now** (`Length`, `Width`, `DebugPlayerCount`) — pin physics moved to the new `PinConfig`. `PinDeck.Initialize` now takes a `PinConfig` instead of a `LaneConfig`.
- **Bowling ball now starts at chest height.** Added a `SpawnHeight` tunable to `BallConfig` (default `1.3` m) and pointed `GreyboxSceneBuilder`'s BallSpawn at it, so the ball drops onto the lane when thrown. Height is now editable in the Inspector without code.
